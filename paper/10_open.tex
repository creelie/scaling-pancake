\section{What remains open}\label{sec:open}

\subsection{The corner of the trapezoid}
Theorem B covers the critical side $P_1P_2$ of the trapezoid of \cite[(1.5)]{BRS25} except the corner
$P_2$, where $q=q_{\rm opt}(p)$. For $p=p_{\rm ST}$, that is $\lambda=\frac{n-1}{2n+2}$, the corner is
reached in \cite[Theorem 1.5]{BRS25}, through the $L^2$ restriction theorem; for every other
$p\in(1,\frac{2n}{n+1})$ it is open, in the plane as well \cite[\S1.2]{BRS25}. Three routes to it
from the results of this paper fail, for the following reasons.

\emph{The limit $q\to q_{\rm opt}(p)$.} Theorem \ref{thm:brs} gives $\Sp(p,q)$ for each $q>q_*$ with a
constant that is not bounded as $q\to q_*$, and the sparse forms $\Lambda^*_{p,q}$ decrease as $q$
decreases, so a family of bounds with growing constants gives nothing at $q_*$.

\emph{A stronger Bochner--Riesz input.} For $p\le p_{\rm ST}$ the exponent $q_*(p,p_\circ)$ equals
$q_{\rm opt}(p)$ for every admissible $p_\circ$ by \eqref{eq:qopt}, and the input is the Stein--Tomas
theorem, which was available before \cite{OAI26}. So below $p_{\rm ST}$ the corner is lost in the
summation over scales in the proof of Theorem \ref{thm:brs}, not in the input, and no improvement of
the Bochner--Riesz bounds, including Theorem \ref{thm:BR}, reaches it. For $p>p_{\rm ST}$ the method
needs in addition $\VBR(p,\frac{n-1}{n+1}p')$ at the Knapp line, the endpoint of the conjectured range
of these inequalities; the exponent $r_*(p,p_\circ)$ of Corollary \ref{cor:vbr} reaches that line only in
the limit \eqref{eq:rstarlimit}, and the restriction estimates of \cite{OAI26} are stated in the
interior of their range only.

\emph{Orthogonality.} At $p=p_{\rm ST}$ the corner has $q_{\rm opt}=2$, and the proof of
\cite[Theorem 1.5]{BRS25} uses the orthogonality of the pieces of the decomposition in $L^2$. At
other points of the critical side $q_{\rm opt}(p)\ne2$, and we know of no substitute.

The self-improvement of Section \ref{sec:boundary} does not help either: it acts on the weight, after
the sparse bound has been used, and leaves the unweighted corner unchanged. The corner is therefore a
problem about sparse domination itself, and needs a new idea there.

\subsection{The other sides}
For $0<\lambda<\frac{n-1}2$, sparse bounds on the top side $P_2P_3$ of the trapezoid, away from the
critical line, are open in every dimension except at $\lambda=\frac{n-1}{2n+2}$, where
\cite[Theorem 1.5]{BRS25} gives the whole closed trapezoid. They are a
strengthening of off-diagonal $L^p\to L^r$ bounds for Bochner--Riesz means below the critical index,
and Theorem \ref{thm:BR} does not reach them. By duality the side $P_3P_4$ is equivalent to the side
$P_1P_2$, so Theorem B also gives it, except for the corner $P_3$.

\subsection{The range of the weights}
The exponent $p_1(w)=1+\frac{n-1}{n+1}(1-\frac1\sigma)$ of Theorem C is the one produced by Theorem
\ref{thm:fn} from the sparse bounds of Theorem B; by Theorem \ref{thm:boundary} the range is
$1<p<1+\frac{n-1}{n+1}(1-\frac1{\sigma_w})$ in terms of the supremum $\sigma_w$ of the reverse
H\"older exponents of $w$. Whether it is the sharp range in terms of the reverse
H\"older exponent is not known, as already noted in \cite[\S1.1]{BRS25}; weighted weak-type bounds
fail for $p>1$ for the power weights $|x|^\eps$, $\eps>0$ \cite[\S1.1]{BRS25}, so some restriction of
this kind is necessary.

\subsection{Other domains}
Parts I and II are written for the round sphere. Beltran, Roos and Seeger work with the Minkowski
functional $\rho$ of a convex domain with smooth boundary of positive curvature, and their
Theorem \ref{thm:brs} holds in that generality. The weak-type endpoint for such $\rho$ in dimensions
$n\ge3$ would follow in the same way from the Bochner--Riesz conjecture for $(1-\rho(\xi))^\lambda_+$,
which the local smoothing argument of Part I does not address.

\subsection{Maximal functions}
The maximal operator $\sup_{t>0}|R^{\lambda(p)}_{a,t}f|$ is not treated here. Already for
$\lambda>\lambda(p)$ and $p<2$ its $L^p$ boundedness requires conditions beyond those of the
Bochner--Riesz conjecture \cite{Tao02}, and nothing in this paper bears on it.
